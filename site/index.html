    How it works

    FiledFor checks about 2,600 company job boards on Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday and SmartRecruiters every 30 minutes. It keeps tech roles in the US that look entry level, reads each description for the years of experience it asks for, and leaves out postings that rule out sponsorship, say citizens only, or need a security clearance.

    Each company is matched to its Department of Labor filings. These are Labor Condition Applications, the step every H-1B petition starts with. The numbers cover certified filings decided between October 2024 and June 2026, counted for the same kind of role as the job (software, AI/ML or data).

    Filings
    How many H-1B applications the company filed for this kind of role.
    New hires
    Filings for new employment, as opposed to extensions or transfers. This is the closest sign of hiring people who need a first H-1B.
    Wage level I-II
    The share filed at the two lower prevailing wage levels, where most new grads land. From FY2027 the H-1B lottery gives higher wage levels more entries, so this mix is worth knowing.

    Limits

    • Filings show what a company did before. They aren't a promise for any one job, so check the posting and ask the recruiter. This isn't legal advice.
    • Companies hiring through other systems, like Oracle or iCIMS, aren't covered yet. On Workday, new jobs show up within 30 minutes, but a closed job can stay listed for up to half a day.
    • Level and sponsorship flags come from rules that read the title and description. They get most jobs right and some wrong.
    • Some companies file under a different legal name. If one shows no filings, it may still sponsor.
